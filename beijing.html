Beijing Travel Guide: What to See, Eat and Do in 2026

Last updated: June 2026 · 10 min read

Beijing is one of those cities that takes you completely by surprise. You expect history — and you get it, in overwhelming quantities — but what catches most visitors off guard is how alive the city feels. Ancient hutong alleys sit next to gleaming subway stations. Street food vendors set up beside Michelin-starred restaurants. It's a city that refuses to be just one thing. Operating entirely on a strict real-name verification network, navigating the Chinese capital seamlessly requires proactive logistics, dynamic routing, and an understanding of municipal systems.

1. Pre-Trip Planning & Essential Logistics

Real-Name Identification Guidelines: Your physical documentation is your lifeline in Beijing. A national identity card or an original valid passport is strictly required for hotel check-ins, ticket verifications, and public security checkpoints across the entire city. Moving around without physical ID is impossible. If applicable, ensure you carry official student, senior citizen, military, or disability credentials, as most state-backed scenic areas offer a 50% discount tier or completely free admission based on these documents. It is highly recommended to store clear digital photos of your ID on your smartphone as an emergency backup.

Vital Mobile Apps Setup: Download and configure a few necessary applications before departure. Prioritize **YiTongXing (亿通行)**, the definitive transit application that lets you scan uniform QR codes instantly at all subway turnstiles and bus gates without buying standalone tokens. Pair this with **Amap (高德地图)** or **Baidu Maps** for accurate lane-level walking and transit routing. Standard mobile payment networks via WeChat Pay or Alipay are universally supported; carrying mass cash is unnecessary. Keeping 50 to 100 RMB in small physical bills is plenty for minor emergency edge cases.

Gear Selection and Daily Packing: Exploring imperial heritage sites involves miles of walking over stone paths, making highly supportive, broken-in athletic footwear an absolute necessity. Pack clothing based on seasonal climate patterns. Beijing has distinct temperature shifts; spring and autumn feature pleasant days but chilly nights requiring an extra jacket layer. Summer brings heavy UV exposure and sudden downpours—bring quality sunscreens, hats, and umbrellas. Winter brings a piercing dry cold, making thick down jackets, thermal layers, scarves, and gloves mandatory. Always pack a reliable power bank, standard digestive medicine, and blister bandages. Carrying a reusable water bottle is smart, as free drinking water stations are readily accessible across metro terminals and major scenic parks.

ROUTING STRATEGY TIP

Map out and group your target destinations by geographical proximity beforehand. Combining nearby historic spots into single-day segments cuts down on commuting and stops you from backtracking across the city's vast multi-ring layout.

2. Core Survival Guidelines & Anti-Scam Rules

The Real-Name Advanced Booking Rule: Almost all world-class historical landmarks in Beijing enforce fixed daily capacity caps and mandatory online bookings. Manual ticket booths on-site do not stock same-day tickets once allocations are fully booked. Never show up at an entrance assuming you can purchase a ticket at the gate. Additionally, keep in mind that the Forbidden City, the National Museum, the Chairman Mao Memorial Hall, and most public galleries are **permanently closed on Mondays**. Schedule your itinerary around this window to avoid arriving to closed gates.

Bypassing Ticket Scalper Fraud: Aggressive ticket touts and unauthorized private tour operators frequently loiter around transport hubs, Tiananmen Square, or Great Wall arrival points. They often pitch fraudulent shortcuts like "guaranteed entry lanes," "exclusive internal ticket blocks," or "zero-queue access." These are predatory schemes designed to extort inflated fees or hand over useless credentials. Use only official municipal mini-programs and verified ticketing channels.

Navigating Public Security Protocols: Security perimeters flanking Tiananmen Square, the Forbidden City, and the Chairman Mao Memorial Hall mirror standard airport checks. Pocket lighters, pocket knives, matches, and large-format pressurized aerosol cans are prohibited and will be confiscated. Travel light to clear these security lines quickly, and keep a close eye on your smartphones and bags in crowded spots.

3. Major Attraction Booking Manual

Open public lifestyle zones—including historic hutongs like Nanluoguxiang, Yandai Xie Street, and the scenic Shichahai lakefronts—are completely open and do not require reservations. However, core heritage landmarks require strict compliance with the following ticketing windows:

Immersive cultural perspectives and imperial architecture preserved across the historic capital.
  • The Forbidden City (The Palace Museum): The most competitive ticket in Beijing. Reservations open exactly **7 days in advance at 8:00 PM (20:00) sharp** via the official "Palace Museum" WeChat mini-program. Tickets are allocated into morning and afternoon entry slots. Morning access is highly recommended for lighter crowds and optimal photography. Tickets cost 60 RMB during peak season. Closed Mondays.
  • Tiananmen Square & Flag Raising Ceremony: Entry is free, but registration is mandatory. Bookings can be made 1 to 7 days ahead via the "Tiananmen Square Reservation" mini-program. To catch the sunrise flag-raising ceremony, select the designated morning slot and arrive at least 60 minutes early to handle the massive crowds and secure a clear view.
  • Chairman Mao Memorial Hall: Free admission. Reservations open 1 to 6 days in advance at **12:30 PM** daily via its specialized mini-program. The hall is strictly open during morning hours and is closed every Monday. Visitors must maintain solemn silence inside.
  • National Museum of China: Free admission. Bookings open 1 to 7 days in advance at **5:00 PM (17:00)** daily via its official platform. Closed Mondays. The museum is massive; plan for at least a half-day visit to see the exhibits properly.
  • Badaling Great Wall: Offers the longest booking window, opening slots up to **15 days in advance at 8:00 AM** daily via its official site. Because allocations sell out quickly during holidays, starting your journey early in the morning is essential for a smooth visit.
  • The Summer Palace, Temple of Heaven, & Prince Gong's Mansion: These historical parks feature relaxed capacities. Booking 3 to 5 days ahead on their official accounts is sufficient. Arrive during early morning hours to see local residents practicing Tai Chi or flying kites before the afternoon tour crowds arrive.

4. Culinary Footprint: The Capital's Flavors

1. Imperial Classics & Sit-Down Landmarks

Authentic Peking Roast Duck: This iconic dish features incredibly crispy skin dipped lightly in sugar, followed by tender meat wrapped in thin wheat pancakes with sweet bean sauce, julienned scallions, and fresh cucumbers. Skip the overpriced, low-quality tourist joints near scenic gates. Instead, choose reliable local institutions like **Siji Minfu (四季民福)** for its crispy texture and excellent value, or **Bianyifang (便宜坊)** for its traditional closed-oven roasting technique. The leftover duck frame can be turned into a rich broth or fried with salt-and-pepper spices at the end of your meal.

Old Beijing Copper-Pot Mutton Shabu-Shabu: A traditional clear-broth hot pot boiled inside a coal-fired copper chimney pot. This clean style emphasizes the premium quality of fresh, hand-cut mutton slices dipped in rich savory sesame paste and paired with sweet pickled garlic. Highly verified local venues include **Nanmen Shuanrou (南门涮肉)** and the historic **Jubaoyuan (聚宝源)** located in the Niujie district.

2. Traditional Street Eats & Local Snacks

For a daily staple, try **Zhajiangmian**, thick wheat noodles tossed with crisp seasonal vegetable toppings and a rich, savory fried pork sauce (vetted spots include *Fangzhuanchang No. 69* or *Haiwanju*). Adventurous foodies should try **Luzhu Huoshao**, a rich stew of pork intestines, lungs, and firm tofu over baked flatbread seasoned with garlic purée and chili oil (*Menkuang Hutong* or *Xiaochangchen*). For traditional desserts, try **BaoDu** (quick-blanched tripe dipped in sesame sauce via *Baodu Feng*), **Wandouhuang** (chilled sweet pea cake), **Lvdagun** (glutinous rice rolls with roasted bean powder), or **Miancha** (millet mush enjoyed traditionally by sipping directly from the bowl's rim without utensils). These snacks are easily found at *Huguosi Snacks* or *Jinfang Snacks* hubs.

Authentic culinary traditions ranging from imperial legacy roasts to classic local comfort dishes.

3. Targeted Gourmet Neighborhoods

Avoid the commercialized Wangfujing Snack Street, which serves as an overpriced tourist hub with subpar flavors. For real culinary heritage, head to **Niujie**, the historic center for authentic Halal dining, premium fresh mutton, and sticky rice cakes. Walk along **Huguosi Street** to find a high density of classic snack counters, or visit **Qianmen and Xianyukou** for reliable sit-down dining options near the city center. Trendy neighborhoods like **Nanluoguxiang and Yandai Xiejie** blend historic courtyard scenery with creative modern street food stalls, making them perfect for casual exploring and photography.

5. Public Transit Infrastructures: Commuting Guidelines

The Subway First Directive: The municipal subway system is your fastest and most predictable transit option in Beijing. It runs completely immune to surface traffic jams and directly links to nearly every major landmark. Fares are metered by distance, ranging affordably from 3 to 7 RMB per trip (with specialized airport express lines flat-rated at 25 RMB). Use your smartphone to pass through turnstiles seamlessly via the **YiTongXing** app or linked payment codes.

Surface Bus Operations: Standard municipal buses cost a flat rate of 1 to 2 RMB per trip. Keep this critical rule in mind: you must tap or scan your transit app **both when boarding through the front door and right before exiting through the rear door**. Forgetting the exit scan causes the system to log an incomplete trip and penalize your account with the maximum route fare deduction.

Rush Hour Commuter Tactics: On working weekdays, the morning rush (**07:30 to 09:00**) and evening peak (**17:00 to 19:00**) bring heavy congestion across major transit lines. Try to schedule your transit windows outside these hours if you don't have a tight booking deadline. Major multi-line interchange stations feature long walking transfers between platforms, so give yourself extra connection time. To visit the Badaling Great Wall safely, avoid illegal black cars soliciting rides on the street. Instead, take the official **Suburban Railway S2 Line** trains or use authorized tourist express shuttle buses departing from central transit squares.

6. Strategic Lodging Guide: Optimal Districts

The core rule for picking accommodations in Beijing is to choose a property located within a short walking distance of a metro station entrance. This simple step cuts down on daily walking and ensures fast transfers across the city. The primary hospitality zones are categorized below:

Qianmen / Dashilan District

The premier choice for first-time travelers. This central area sits just south of Tiananmen Square and the Forbidden City, placing major historical sites within easy walking distance or a short, direct ride away. The vibrant historic neighborhood features busy pedestrian streets and a wide array of lodging choices, from budget-friendly international chains to boutique hotels.

Dongsi / Nanluoguxiang District

Perfect for travelers looking for an authentic local atmosphere. This historic neighborhood is famous for its preserved courtyard architecture and quiet residential lanes. The surrounding side streets are filled with independent cafes, boutique shops, and local eateries. It offers excellent metro links to the city center, with lodging options centered around stylish courtyard guesthouses.

Xizhimen Transit Hub Area

A major transportation gateway in the northwestern part of the city. As a key multi-line metro junction and railway terminal, it offers fast, direct transit links to all major attractions and outer scenic areas. The neighborhood features a high concentration of dependable domestic business hotels that offer great value and practical convenience for budget-conscious travelers.

Guomao / Sanlitun District

The city's premier commercial and financial center. The skyline is defined by modern skyscrapers, luxury shopping malls, upscale dining, and a lively nightlife scene. Accommodations here consist mainly of premium international luxury hotels with higher rates. Because it sits further east from the core historic sites, it is best suited for business travelers or visitors prioritizing shopping and evening entertainment.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY / LOGISTICAL TAKEAWAYS

Navigating Beijing successfully depends on smart preparation: always carry your physical passport or ID card, register for core historic sites exactly 7 days in advance online, select a hotel room positioned near a metro platform, and use digital transit apps to travel efficiently. With your logistics sorted in advance, you can fully immerse yourself in the incredible historic scale of the capital!
